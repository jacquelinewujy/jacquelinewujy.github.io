About Me

I’ve always been curious about why people behave the way they do—and why everyday products sometimes make simple things harder than they need to be. Even something as small as struggling to get the last bit of lotion out of a bottle makes me wonder how it could work better.

At Tufts, I combine psychology and engineering to understand people and improve the products and systems around them. I enjoy turning messy problems into clearer ones: asking questions, finding patterns, and figuring out what actually matters to the people using something.

My background in architecture, marketing, and media also shapes how I approach problems. It has taught me to think about form, function, constraints, visual communication, audience behavior, and storytelling—giving me multiple ways to understand both a problem and the people experiencing it.
